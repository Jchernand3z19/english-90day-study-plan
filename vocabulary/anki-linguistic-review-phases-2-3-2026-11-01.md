# Anki linguistic review — Phases 2 and 3

**Review date:** 2026-11-01  
**Scope:** 660 English phrase / Spanish translation pairs in the ordered APKG: Phase 2 (positions 341–695; 355 cards) and Phase 3 (696–1000; 305 cards).  
**Method:** direct review of the English field and Spanish translation, with attention to grammar, naturalness in Latin American Spanish, semantic equivalence, placeholders, register, and whether a phrase is a complete sentence or a context-dependent fragment.

## Result

The 660 pairs were reviewed. Most are understandable and usable as vocabulary prompts. The entries below are candidates for correction or added context; they are not all equally severe. Some are genuine English-form errors, while others are grammatical but unnatural, region-specific, overly literal, or ambiguous without context.

**No APKG, CSV manifest, or live Anki collection was modified.** The original and ordered packages remain unchanged. Recommendations below are editorial proposals only.

### Phase 2 notes that do not require automatic correction

- “Keep the change” translated with “quédese” uses formal address; keep it if the scenario is a polite interaction with a driver or service worker.
- “Bless you!” → “¡Salud!” is appropriate specifically after a sneeze; retain the context note.
- “I’ve been robbed” → “Me han robado” is valid, though “Me asaltaron” may fit some Latin American contexts more naturally.
- “May I?” → “¿Puedo?” is context-dependent; the note about formality is useful.
- “Let’s have a drink” can refer to a non-alcoholic drink too; do not assume alcohol.
- “I’m invited to a party” and emotion adjectives require gender agreement in Spanish; slash forms are acceptable as learning notation but can be made clearer in examples.

## Cross-phase editorial rules

1. Use natural Honduran/Latin American Spanish as the default, while retaining Spain-specific alternatives only when explicitly labeled.
2. Keep English target phrases fully in English; use English placeholders such as “[name]”, “[country]”, and “[word]”.
3. Distinguish a complete sentence from an idiom, fragment, or sentence starter. Give fragments a short model sentence where needed.
4. Avoid literal translations that are technically understandable but unlikely to be used naturally in conversation.
5. Preserve gender/register variants only when they teach a real distinction; otherwise use a neutral formulation or a short note.
6. Do not silently alter the deck. Review proposed changes first, then create a new version only with learner approval.
