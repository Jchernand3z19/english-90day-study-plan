# Day 089 — Final integrated assessment

**Phase:** 3 — Fluency, discussion, and nuance  
**Estimated time:** 60 minutes  
**Status:** Pending  
**Objective:** demonstrate current practical ability in listening, conversation, opinions, and professional communication, then identify what needs more practice.

This is an internal progress check, not an official CEFR exam. Use your current ability; do not memorise a script or invent evidence.

## Assessment rules

- Complete the tasks in English to the best of your ability. Instructions and feedback may be in Spanish.
- Use keywords only for speaking tasks; do not read a full prepared answer.
- For listening, have a tutor or text-to-speech tool read the script in Section 3 without showing it to you first. If this is not possible, record that listening was not assessed rather than pretending it was.
- Record your actual answers and score. The tutor should give feedback after each task, but should not supply your answer in advance.

## 1. Preparation and retrieval — 5 minutes

Review the phase's target phrases briefly. Close your notes and say five phrases you can use to give an opinion, explain a past event, handle a problem, or ask for clarification. This warm-up is not scored.

## 2. Opinion and conversation — 15 minutes

**Task A — Opinion (10 points)**

Speak for 60–90 seconds about this question:

**Is it better to learn a new skill alone or with other people?**

- Give your opinion.
- Explain two reasons.
- Give one example.
- Use at least two connectors, such as **because**, **for example**, **however**, or **also**.

**Task B — Follow-up conversation (10 points)**

Ask a tutor or partner to disagree politely with one of your ideas. Respond respectfully, explain your thinking, and ask one relevant follow-up question. If practising alone, record your response to this prompt: “Some people learn better alone because they can concentrate.”

## 3. Listening and paraphrasing — 10 minutes

Ask a tutor or text-to-speech tool to read the script below twice without showing it first. Then answer the questions aloud.

**Listening script — reveal only after the two listens**

> Alex: I used to study English only by memorising words.  
> Nina: Did that help?  
> Alex: It helped me recognise some words, but I couldn't answer quickly in conversations.  
> Nina: What changed?  
> Alex: I started listening to short dialogues and repeating useful sentences. I also practised speaking for ten minutes every day.  
> Nina: Do you feel fluent now?  
> Alex: Not completely, but I can explain simple ideas more easily. I still need to improve my listening.

**Questions**
1. What did Alex do at first?
2. What problem did Alex have?
3. Name two things Alex changed.
4. What skill does Alex still want to improve?

**Task C — Listening (10 points):** score one point for each correct answer, then double the result to convert it to 10. After answering, summarise Alex's experience in one or two English sentences.

## 4. Professional communication and repair — 15 minutes

**Task D — Professional introduction (10 points)**

Give a 45–60 second introduction for a networking event or interview. Include your real experience, one strength, and the kind of work or skill you want to develop. Keep claims truthful.

**Task E — Unexpected question (10 points)**

Answer this question aloud: **“Tell me about a problem you had and how you handled it.”** Use a real example if possible. A clearly labelled hypothetical example is acceptable. Include what happened, what you did, and the result or lesson. If you do not understand the prompt, practise saying **“Could you rephrase the question, please?”**

## 5. Score and review — 15 minutes

Score the five tasks using the assessment rubric below. For each task, record one strength and one correction. Retry one corrected sentence.

| Task | Maximum points | Score |
|---|---:|---:|
| A — Opinion | 10 | Not assessed |
| B — Conversation | 10 | Not assessed |
| C — Listening | 10 | Not assessed |
| D — Professional introduction | 10 | Not assessed |
| E — Unexpected question | 10 | Not assessed |
| **Total** | **50** | **Not assessed** |

Convert to a percentage: **score earned ÷ 50 × 100**. Suggested passing score: **80%**, with every task attempted and the main message understandable. If a task could not be assessed, mark it **Not assessed**; do not assign zero evidence or claim a pass without completing it. If below 80%, mark **Review**, identify the two weakest skills, and practise them before repeating a similar assessment.

## Evidence and completion

Record the date, actual time spent, task answers or summaries, listening method, score, corrections, and next review target in your daily log. Keep this lesson **Pending** until started; use **In progress** while working, **Review** if more practice is needed, and **Approved** only when evidence meets the rubric.

Day 90 is for reflection and a realistic next-30-day plan. This internal assessment does not certify a CEFR level or guarantee B1/B2.
