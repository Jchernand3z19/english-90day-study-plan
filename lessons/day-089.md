# Day 089 — Final integrated assessment

**Phase:** 3 — Fluency, discussion, and nuance  
**Estimated time:** 60 minutes  
**Status:** Pending  
**Objective:** demonstrate current practical ability in listening, conversation, opinions, and professional communication, then identify what needs more practice.

This is an internal progress check, not an official CEFR exam. Use your current ability; do not memorise a script or invent evidence.

## Assessment rules

- Complete speaking tasks aloud, without reading a full script.
- Use keywords only for speaking tasks.
- For listening, have a tutor or text-to-speech tool read the script in Section 3 without showing it to you first. If this is not possible, record listening as **Not assessed** rather than pretending it was.
- Record actual answers and scores. Feedback should come after each task, not before.

## 1. Preparation and retrieval — 5 minutes

Close your notes and say five phrases you can use to give an opinion, explain a past event, handle a problem, or ask for clarification. This warm-up is not scored.

## 2. Task A — Opinion with support (10 minutes, 20 points)

Speak for 60–90 seconds about this question:

**Is it better to learn a new skill alone or with other people?**

- Give your opinion.
- Explain two reasons.
- Give one example.
- Use at least two connectors, such as **because**, **for example**, **however**, or **also**.

Score clarity, relevant support, use of connectors, and ability to communicate without reading a full script.

## 3. Task B — Conversation and response (10 minutes, 20 points)

Respond to this different viewpoint respectfully: **“Some people learn better alone because they can concentrate.”** Explain your response and ask one relevant follow-up question. A tutor or AI partner can disagree with one point and ask you to respond.

Score whether you respond to the other idea, explain your position, and keep the exchange understandable.

## 4. Task C — Professional scenario (10 minutes, 20 points)

Give a 45–60 second professional introduction for a networking event or interview. Include your real experience, one strength, and a skill you want to develop. Then answer two questions:
1. What kind of work do you enjoy?
2. Can you describe a problem you solved or a task you handled?

Keep claims truthful. Score relevance, clarity, examples, and ability to answer follow-up questions.

## 5. Task D — Listening and paraphrasing (10 minutes, 20 points)

Ask a tutor or text-to-speech tool to read the script below twice without showing it first. Answer the questions, summarise the main idea in one or two English sentences, and paraphrase one specific idea from the dialogue in your own words.

****Listening script — reveal only after two listens**

> Alex: I used to study English only by memorising words.  
> Nina: Did that help?  
> Alex: It helped me recognise some words, but I couldn't answer quickly in conversations.  
> Nina: What changed?  
> Alex: I started listening to short dialogues and repeating useful sentences. I also practised speaking for ten minutes every day.  
> Nina: Do you feel fluent now?  
> Alex: Not completely, but I can explain simple ideas more easily. I still need to improve my listening.

**Questions**
1. What did Alex do at first?
2. What problem did Alex have?
3. Name two things Alex changed.
4. What skill does Alex still want to improve?

Score comprehension of the main idea and details, plus the accuracy of your short summary. If listening was not actually performed, mark this task **Not assessed**.

## 6. Task E — Conversation repair (10 minutes, 20 points)

Answer this unexpected question aloud: **“Tell me about a problem you had and how you handled it.”** Use a real example if possible; a clearly labelled hypothetical example is acceptable. Include what happened, what you did, and the result or lesson. If you do not understand the prompt, practise asking **“Could you rephrase the question, please?”** Then reformulate one sentence more clearly.

Score whether you explain the situation, ask for clarification when needed, and make the message clearer on a second attempt.

## Scoring and review — 5 minutes

| Task | Maximum points | Score |
|---|---:|---:|
| A — Opinion with support | 20 | Not assessed |
| B — Conversation and response | 20 | Not assessed |
| C — Professional scenario | 20 | Not assessed |
| D — Listening and paraphrasing | 20 | Not assessed |
| E — Conversation repair | 20 | Not assessed |
| **Total** | **100** | **Not assessed** |

Record actual scores only after completing the tasks. Suggested passing score: **80/100**, with every task attempted and the main message understandable. If any task was not assessed, mark the checkpoint **Incomplete**; do not claim a pass. If below 80, mark **Review**, identify the two weakest skills, and practise them before repeating a similar assessment.

For each task, record one strength and one correction. Retry one corrected sentence.

## Evidence and completion

Record the date, actual time spent, task answers or summaries, listening method, score, corrections, and next review target in your daily log. Keep this lesson **Pending** until started; use **In progress** while working, **Review** if more practice is needed, and **Approved** only when evidence meets the assessment rules.

Day 90 is for reflection and a realistic next-30-day plan. This internal assessment does not certify a CEFR level or guarantee B1/B2.
