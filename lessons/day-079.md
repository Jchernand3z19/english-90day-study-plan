# Day 079 — Mock job interview

**Phase:** 3 — Fluency, discussion, and nuance  
**Estimated time:** 60 minutes  
**Status:** Pending  
**Objective:** answer common interview questions clearly in simple professional English, using truthful examples and asking for clarification when needed.

## 1. Anki and retrieval — 10 minutes

Review due cards from your 1,000 common English phrases deck. Say each phrase before revealing it. Choose up to five difficult phrases and use each in a new sentence. Record actual counts; do not estimate progress.

## 2. Interview language — 15 minutes

Practise the phrases below. Replace the examples with information that is true for you. Do not invent qualifications, results, or work experience.

| English phrase | Spanish meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Thank you for inviting me.** | Gracias por invitarme. | Thank you for inviting me to this interview. |
| **I have experience in…** | Tengo experiencia en… | I have experience in reporting and data analysis. |
| **One of my strengths is…** | Una de mis fortalezas es… | One of my strengths is solving problems. |
| **In my previous role, I…** | En mi puesto anterior, yo… | In my previous role, I prepared reports. |
| **I learned how to…** | Aprendí a… | I learned how to organize information more efficiently. |
| **Could you repeat the question, please?** | ¿Podría repetir la pregunta, por favor? | Could you repeat the question, please? |
| **Could you tell me more about the role?** | ¿Podría contarme más sobre el puesto? | Could you tell me more about the role? |

**Answer structure:** give a direct answer, add one detail or example, then stop. Short, clear answers are better than memorized speeches.

## 3. Listening and shadowing — 15 minutes

Ask a tutor or text-to-speech tool to read the interview exchange below **without showing you the text first**. Listen twice and answer the questions. If studying alone, cover the script while using a read-aloud feature.

**Listening script — reveal only after the first two listens**

> Interviewer: Could you tell me a little about yourself?  
> Candidate: Certainly. I have experience in reporting and data analysis. I enjoy turning information into clear reports.  
> Interviewer: What is one of your strengths?  
> Candidate: One of my strengths is attention to detail. I check my work and try to find practical solutions.  
> Interviewer: Can you give me an example?  
> Candidate: In my previous role, I prepared reports to help the team understand business results.  
> Interviewer: Do you have any questions for me?  
> Candidate: Yes. Could you tell me more about the main priorities for this role?

**Comprehension questions**
1. What kind of work experience does the candidate mention?
2. What strength does the candidate identify?
3. What example does the candidate give?
4. What does the candidate ask the interviewer?

Check the script only after answering. Shadow two lines and notice the stress in **experience**, **strength**, and **priorities**.

## 4. Speaking production — 15 minutes

Answer these questions aloud. Aim for 20–40 seconds per answer; use keywords, not a complete script.

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. What is one of your strengths?
3. Describe a work task or project you handled.
4. Why are you interested in this role?
5. What question would you ask the interviewer?

Use your real experience. If you do not have experience related to a hypothetical role, say what you have done that is relevant or what you are willing to learn—do not claim experience you do not have.

If using an AI tutor, ask it to act as an interviewer and present one question at a time. Request corrections in Spanish after each answer, then repeat the improved answer once without reading.

## 5. Self-check — 5 minutes

- Answer at least four interview questions aloud.
- Use at least three phrases from the table appropriately.
- Answer at least three of the four listening questions correctly.
- Ask one relevant question or request clarification if needed.

**Suggested pass:** at least 8/10 using the daily rubric, with an oral attempt and truthful, understandable answers. If you need to read every answer, mark **Review** and retry after targeted practice.

Record the date, actual time spent, Anki cards reviewed, listening method, questions answered, score, corrections, and next review target in your daily log. Keep the lesson **Pending** until started; use **In progress** while working, **Review** if more practice is needed, and **Approved** only when evidence meets the rubric.
