# Day 070 — Formal and informal language

**Phase:** 3 — Fluency, discussion, and nuance  
**Estimated time:** 60 minutes  
**Status:** Pending  
**Objective:** choose polite, natural language for casual conversations and professional situations.

## 1. Phrase retrieval — 10 minutes

Choose 5–10 useful phrases from this lesson or a previous one. Try to say each before looking, then use two in new answers. Record what you recalled without help; no deck is required for this step.

## 2. Choose the right register — 15 minutes

| Informal | More formal / polite | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Can you help me?** | **Could you help me, please?** | ¿Puede ayudarme, por favor? |
| **I want…** | **I'd like…** | Quiero… / Quisiera… |
| **Tell me…** | **Could you tell me…?** | Dígame… / ¿Podría decirme…? |
| **Sorry, what?** | **Sorry, could you repeat that, please?** | Perdón, ¿podría repetirlo? |
| **Thanks!** | **Thank you for your help.** | ¡Gracias! / Gracias por su ayuda. |
| **See you!** | **Goodbye. Have a good day.** | ¡Nos vemos! / Adiós, que tenga un buen día. |

Formal does not always mean better. Use informal language with friends when it feels natural; use polite forms with unfamiliar people, service staff, and professional contacts. Avoid slang you do not understand.

**Practice:** Convert these into polite requests:
1. “Give me the report.”
2. “Tell me the time.”
3. “I want more information.”
Then decide which register fits: texting a friend, speaking to a hotel receptionist, emailing a recruiter.

## 3. Listening and shadowing — 15 minutes

Ask a tutor or text-to-speech tool to read both mini-dialogues without showing them first.

> **Casual:**  
> Mia: Hey, can you send me the address?  
> Ben: Sure, I'll send it now. Thanks for reminding me.
>
> **Professional:**  
> Mia: Good morning. Could you send me the address when you have a moment?  
> Ben: Certainly. I'll send it shortly. Thank you for checking.

Questions:
1. Which dialogue is more formal?
2. What request appears in both dialogues?
3. Name one expression that makes the second dialogue more polite.
4. In which situation would you use the casual version?

Check the script after answering, then shadow one line from each dialogue. If you did not actually hear the dialogues, mark listening **Not assessed**; reading the script is not listening evidence.

## 4. Speaking production — 15 minutes

Perform the same request in two situations:
- Ask a friend to send you a location.
- Ask a professional contact to send you a meeting location.

Keep the meaning the same but change the register. Then role-play asking a receptionist for help politely. An AI tutor can correct tone and wording in Spanish after each attempt.

## 5. Self-check — 5 minutes

- Convert at least three direct phrases into polite requests.
- Explain when to use formal versus informal language.
- Answer at least three listening questions correctly.
- Complete both versions of the role-play aloud.

**Suggested pass:** at least 8/10 on the daily rubric, with an actual listening attempt. If both versions sound identical or the request is unclear, mark **Review**.
