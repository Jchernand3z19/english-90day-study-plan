# Integrated listening and speaking architecture

**Status:** Design requirements defined; 90-lesson text audit completed on 2026-11-01. The learner pilot began partially on 2026-11-01 and remains incomplete. Full Anki-to-lesson traceability and human audio-quality review remain pending.  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-01

## Purpose

The course must develop the ability to understand real spoken English and communicate understandably and increasingly spontaneously. Knowing a written phrase or completing an Anki card is not evidence that the learner can recognise that phrase in speech or use it in conversation.

This document defines one connected learning system for all 90 days. It does not create a parallel curriculum or require additional resources for every lesson.

## One learning system, one daily lesson

The 90-day curriculum is the planning source of truth. Each daily lesson owns its objective, target language, practice sequence, and evidence. Other resources serve that lesson:

- **Lesson:** teaches and organises the target language.
- **Anki:** retrieves selected phrases and supports spaced recall; it does not define a second syllabus.
- **Audio:** provides actual listening exposure and a model of spoken language.
- **Speaking practice:** turns recognised language into usable responses and conversation.
- **Assessment:** identifies what is understood, pronounced, and produced independently.
- **Tracker:** preserves evidence and carries unresolved learning needs into later lessons.

Do not add a resource unless it supports a stated lesson objective. Avoid duplicate exercises and disconnected resource banks.

## Cumulative learning

A lesson being completed does not mean all its language is permanently mastered. Later lessons must reuse earlier content in meaningful new contexts.

Use five mechanisms:

1. **Incorporation:** introduce a manageable amount of new vocabulary, expressions, and grammar.
2. **Reuse:** retrieve useful earlier phrases and structures.
3. **Expansion:** extend familiar language with a new tense, detail, connector, or function when appropriate.
4. **Variation:** use known language in a different situation, with a different speaker, question, or response.
5. **Retrieval:** ask the learner to recall and use language without seeing the answer.

Track mastery separately from lesson completion:
- **Introduced:** explained or encountered; not yet reliable.
- **In practice:** recognised or used with prompts, repetition, or support.
- **Consolidated:** understood and used independently on more than one occasion and, where relevant, in a new context.

A lesson can be complete while some phrases or skills remain In practice. Revisit those items later without repeating the whole lesson.

## Spoken-English skill strands

Teach these progressively, not all at once:

### Listening and speech recognition
- Word boundaries and connected speech: recognising where one word ends and the next begins.
- Weak forms and reduced function words: especially articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, and pronouns.
- Contractions: recognising and gradually using common standard forms.
- Informal reductions: recognising common forms such as *gonna* and *wanna* when appropriate; production is optional until the learner is ready.
- Stress and rhythm: identifying content words, sentence stress, and thought groups.
- Intonation: recognising question patterns, emphasis, attitude, and completion.
- Sound contrasts: distinguishing phonemes that change meaning.
- Speech rate and variation: progressing from clear, controlled speech to more natural speed and a range of familiar voices/accents.
- Context and discourse: using situation, grammar, and conversational cues to infer meaning, including hesitation, repetition, and self-correction.

### Pronunciation and spoken production
- Individual sounds and intelligibility, prioritising contrasts that affect understanding.
- Word stress, sentence stress, rhythm, and intonation.
- Linking and natural transitions between words.
- Chunking: speaking in meaningful groups rather than isolated words.
- Automatic retrieval: producing familiar sentence patterns without translating each word.
- Conversation skills: responding to meaning, asking follow-up questions, clarifying, repairing misunderstandings, paraphrasing, and taking turns.
- Fluency: gradually extending responses while keeping them understandable; speed is not the primary measure.

Do not demand a native accent. Prioritise intelligibility, listening recognition, and successful communication.

## Phrase-to-speech learning loop

For a selected target phrase or useful language chunk, connect the following steps when suitable for the lesson:

1. **Meaning and form:** understand the written phrase, its function, and a short example.
2. **Sound recognition:** hear the phrase in real or appropriately modelled speech without reading first.
3. **Notice:** identify relevant contractions, weak forms, linking, stress, rhythm, or intonation. Explain only the features that matter for that phrase.
4. **Imitation:** repeat a short chunk, then the complete phrase; use slower and natural models where available.
5. **Response:** answer a question or use the phrase in a guided exchange.
6. **Transfer:** retrieve and use it in a different context or with a different conversational prompt.
7. **Later retrieval:** bring it back in a future lesson or review without repeating the original dialogue verbatim.

Not every phrase needs a separate pronunciation lecture. Select the most useful phrases and the most relevant sound features for the learner's current level.

## Anki, audio, and speaking alignment

The existing ordered Anki deck remains intact; do not re-import or overwrite it.

For each lesson, select a small relevant subset of existing cards where useful, using the category-to-lesson thematic map. Select dynamically rather than assigning a fixed list of cards to every day; this preserves natural conversation and avoids a parallel Anki syllabus. When exact cards are used, record their identity and indicate:
- phrase/card identity and meaning;
- whether it is prior retrieval or newly introduced support;
- relevant audio reference, if available and checked;
- spoken feature to notice, if any;
- listening, imitation, and speaking/transfer activity;
- mastery state and later review need.

Do not assume that the deck's 1,000 audio references have been individually listened to, quality-checked, or aligned to lesson dialogues. The package audit only established that audio references exist and their referenced media was present. Their content, accent, clarity, naturalness, and phrase-to-lesson alignment still require sampling and systematic review.

Keep standard spelling in Anki. Do not respell words phonetically or alter cards to imitate pronunciation. Put pronunciation notes and sound examples in lesson guidance or a linked audio/pronunciation note.

Anki is not a mandatory daily quota. Use only the number of cards that reinforces the current lesson without creating an excessive parallel workload. Do not equate card recall with listening or speaking mastery.

## Audio and evidence integrity

- A listening task requires an audio source that the learner actually hears.
- Listening before seeing the transcript is preferred for the first attempt; replay and transcript review can follow.
- A written dialogue read by the tutor is not automatically equivalent to a recording by a natural speaker. Label the source accurately.
- Reading a transcript, repeating a model, and listening comprehension are different activities and must be recorded separately.
- If no audio is available or played, record listening as **Not assessed**. Continue with speaking or pronunciation work without inventing listening evidence.
- When possible, use audio that contains target lesson language and selected previously learned phrases. Controlled new language is acceptable when it directly serves the objective and remains level-appropriate.
- Do not use an advanced external audio merely to fill a resource slot. Check its topic, level, speed, accent, transcript, and task before assigning it.
- Where possible, expose the learner to more than one clear voice/accent over time, without changing several difficulty variables at once.

## Per-lesson traceability checklist

Before a lesson is considered fully aligned, its file or its linked lesson map should make the following visible:

1. One measurable communication objective.
2. A small set of new target phrases/structures.
3. Selected Anki phrases for retrieval or support, with no duplicate parallel syllabus.
4. Earlier phrases/structures intentionally reused.
5. Actual listening source and the target language it contains; source type and availability stated honestly.
6. One or two relevant spoken features to notice, such as weak forms, linking, stress, intonation, or sound contrast.
7. Guided pronunciation/imitation that does not replace listening.
8. Guided speaking with a model before independent production.
9. A new-context transfer or conversational response.
10. Separate evidence for listening recognition, pronunciation/intelligibility, and spontaneous production.
11. Mastery states and unresolved items to revisit in later lessons.
12. Timing and workload that fit the learner's current level.

If an element is not relevant or no suitable source exists, state that explicitly rather than adding filler.

## Progressive sequence across the 90 days

- **Days 1–30:** clear, short speech; basic sound contrasts; common standard contractions; simple word linking and weak forms; basic sentence stress and question intonation; short listening chunks and supported responses.
- **Days 31–60:** more connected speech; common reductions in recognition; broader stress/intonation patterns; familiar speech at a more natural pace; short narratives, clarification, follow-up questions, and transfer to new situations.
- **Days 61–90:** more spontaneous speech; longer thought groups; varied familiar voices and accents; natural hesitation and repair; summarising, paraphrasing, professional and social conversation. Difficulty remains conditional on demonstrated readiness.

This is a progression guide, not a promise that every feature will be mastered by a fixed day.

## Natural conversation: Anki is a starting point, not a script

Conversation practice must sound and function like a real, coherent interaction. Anki supplies useful language to retrieve and practise; it does not limit the vocabulary, grammar, or expressions that may appear in a conversation, and it is not a script that the learner must recite.

For each conversation:
- Give the exchange a clear communicative purpose and a believable context (for example, getting to know someone, making a plan, explaining a preference, or solving a simple problem).
- Intentionally retrieve only a small, relevant selection of Anki phrases. Do not force unrelated cards into the exchange or attempt to use the whole deck.
- Add the ordinary language needed to make the interaction flow: connectors, reactions, follow-up questions, short explanations, clarification, turn-taking, and context-relevant vocabulary. Examples include *well*, *actually*, *because*, *so*, *by the way*, *Really?*, *That sounds interesting*, and *What about you?*
- Allow answers to lead naturally to the next question. The tutor should respond to the meaning of the learner's answer rather than mechanically advancing through a fixed list of target phrases.
- Keep the language and pace appropriate to the learner's demonstrated level. Natural conversation for a beginner means short, understandable turns with useful support—not an overload of unfamiliar expressions or artificially fast speech.
- Model and explain genuinely new language before expecting the learner to use it independently. Ask one question at a time, allow the learner to answer, then give one or two high-value corrections and invite a retry.
- Reuse familiar Anki language in new contexts so retrieval becomes flexible communication rather than memorised dialogue performance.

New expressions that arise naturally can be recorded as **candidate learning items**. Add them to Anki only after review when they are reusable, useful, level-appropriate, and not redundant with an existing card. Do not create a card for every new word or let card creation overload the learner.

A conversation can therefore contain both selected Anki language and new supporting language. Its success is judged by whether the learner understands and communicates the intended meaning, not by how many deck phrases were inserted. The detailed phrase-to-speech loop remains available for selected target chunks; it does not turn the entire conversation into a phrase drill.
