# Study Method and Rules

## Purpose

This repository is a structured 90-day practice plan for practical conversation. The 90 days are a guide, not a deadline. Progress depends on demonstrated ability, not on the calendar.

## Standard study cycle

1. **Retrieve:** try to remember familiar phrases before looking.
2. **Understand:** learn a small set of useful expressions in context.
3. **Listen:** hear a supplied dialogue or suitable audio without reading the script first.
4. **Imitate:** repeat short chunks, focusing on rhythm and word stress.
5. **Produce:** answer a question or perform a role-play without reading a full script.
6. **Correct:** note one to three meaningful errors.
7. **Retry:** use the corrected phrase in a new answer.
8. **Review later:** retrieve the phrase again in a later session.

## Language balance: Spanish + English

- At the beginning, explain new grammar, confusing expressions, and corrections in Spanish.
- Practise useful phrases in English immediately after understanding the meaning.
- Start with short English answers, then add a detail, a reason, and a follow-up question.
- Gradually reduce Spanish support only when the learner can complete the task comfortably.
- Do not force an all-English lesson before the learner can understand the task.
- Prioritise conversation and interaction over long grammar explanations or isolated vocabulary lists.

## Mastery gate: when to advance

The numbered day is a suggested sequence, not a requirement to move on. Use the following decision after each core lesson:

- **Ready to advance:** the learner can complete the main speaking task with understandable English, retrieve the target phrases with little or no help, and meet the stated check (normally at least 8/10).
- **Needs review:** the main message is unclear, target phrases cannot be recalled or used, or the learner needs to read a complete script. Repeat the core task with simpler prompts and revisit it later.
- **Incomplete / not assessed:** the speaking task or required listening task was not actually attempted. Do not mark the lesson approved.
- **Rest/review day:** use it to revisit the weakest skill; it does not automatically approve a lesson or phase.

Do not require perfect grammar or a native accent. The essential standard is understandable communication and evidence that the target skill can be used, not memorised recitation. If a core prerequisite is not secure, delay the next difficult topic and reinforce the prerequisite first.

## Standard 60-minute session

Use the times in each lesson as guidance. A typical session may include retrieval, target language, listening, speaking, and a short self-check. Review/rest lessons should be shorter and their time allocations should match the stated total.

## How to use an AI tutor

Suggested prompt:

> Act as my patient English conversation tutor. My current level is approximately A1/A2. Prioritise real conversation. Explain difficult points and corrections in Spanish, then help me practise in English. Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer. Do not give me a complete answer before I try. Start with short answers and gradually ask for more detail. If I cannot use a target phrase independently, simplify the prompt and practise it again. Assess the actual response, give one or two corrections, ask me to retry, and do not claim I passed without evidence.

For role-play, tell the tutor the situation and ask it to stay in character. Request feedback after the exchange so the conversation can flow.

## Evidence and honesty

- Record only work that actually happened.
- Distinguish listening to audio from reading text aloud.
- Do not claim a recording exists unless one was made.
- Do not count a phrase as mastered because it was seen once.
- Do not mark a session approved without a completed task and evaluation.
- If a session is missed, keep it pending and resume it later; do not automatically skip it or mark it passed.
- If listening cannot be performed, mark it **Not assessed** rather than inventing a score.

## Anki guidance — setup deferred

The Anki deck has not yet been selected or audited. Until that work is completed, **do not install a random deck and do not let Anki block a lesson**. If a daily lesson asks for Anki cards, use that time to retrieve 5–10 useful phrases from the current or previous lessons without looking, say them aloud, and use two in a new answer. Record this as phrase retrieval, not as Anki progress.

Deck selection, inspection, topic ordering, duplicate checks, and any import/tagging automation are deferred until the 90-day calendar and lesson resources have been validated. The 1,000-phrase target is provisional until an actual deck is inspected.


The target deck contains 1,000 common phrases. Use the deck's real statistics when updating counts. A phrase is considered mastered for this plan only when it can be understood, recalled without looking, used in a new sentence or dialogue, and retrieved again during a later review. This is a practical tracking rule, not a replacement for Anki's scheduling algorithm.

## Level expectations

A1/A2 is the estimated starting range, not a formal diagnosis. The goal is greater independence and conversational confidence. Reaching B1 or B2 in 90 days cannot be guaranteed; progress depends on starting ability, practice quality, listening exposure, and feedback. Use a recognised assessment if you need an official level.
