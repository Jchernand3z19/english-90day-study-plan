# Natural conversation implementation audit — 2026-10-31

**Scope:** focused review of the title/objective, listening dialogue, and main speaking/production task in all 90 daily lesson files.  
**Purpose:** check whether conversation practice uses Anki as a retrieval aid while still developing coherent, natural interaction.  
**Audit status:** all 90 lesson files were opened and their core interaction sections sampled. This is a curriculum-level alignment review, not a line-by-line proofread of every phrase, translation, answer key, grammar explanation, audio file, or external link.

## Overall findings

The curriculum already contains a progression toward natural conversation, especially in Phases 2 and 3. It does not need a wholesale rewrite.

- **Days 1–7:** intentionally controlled beginner exchanges, sentence frames, greetings, and short personal answers. This is appropriate for the learner's starting level. Natural follow-ups should be introduced gently by the tutor, one at a time, after the base phrase is understood.
- **Days 8–13:** everyday service dialogues (shopping, café/restaurant, asking for help) mostly use fixed, short scripts and controlled substitutions. This is suitable for initial practice, but tutor-led speaking should add one relevant response or follow-up when the learner is ready rather than stopping at isolated repetition.
- **Days 15–20:** directions and transport use short scripts and simple models. Day 17 had a particularly heavy production task: four information questions followed by a mandatory role switch and a second performance. This contradicted the beginner workload corrections. Day 17 has now been revised to a small core exchange, one natural follow-up, and optional ticket-purchase/role-switch extensions.
- **Days 22–27:** daily routines, frequency, hobbies, work, and typical-day dialogues provide relevant contexts. The main speaking tasks correctly begin with one or two modelled sentences and progress toward short connected output. Day 24's listening dialogue includes natural follow-up turns; the learner's production demand remains intentionally shorter.
- **Days 31–50:** travel, past events, and problem-solving activities generally include believable exchanges, relevant follow-up questions, clarification, and a manageable core task with optional extensions. These lessons support the transition from controlled phrases to meaningful interaction.
- **Days 52–58:** social conversation is an explicit learning target. The sequence includes opening a conversation, invitations, interests, follow-up questions, reactions, alternatives, and a short social exchange. This is well aligned with the new requirement.
- **Days 61–90:** discussions, opinions, idioms, phrasal verbs, professional introductions, networking, meetings, interviews, paraphrasing, conversation repair, and guided free conversation increasingly require responses based on what the other speaker says. Day 80 explicitly teaches conversation stages rather than a complete script; Day 87 requires a guided free conversation. These are strong examples of the intended direction.

## Decisions and targeted correction

1. Keep the first beginner lessons scaffolded. Do not mistake natural conversation for long, open-ended, or fast dialogue.
2. Use Anki only for a small, relevant set of retrieval targets. Do not force every card into the day's conversation.
3. In tutor-led speaking, let the learner's answer determine the next turn. Add a simple reaction, connector, or follow-up question when appropriate.
4. Introduce new expressions only when they help the exchange; explain/model them before expecting independent use.
5. Keep extensions optional until the core exchange is comfortable.
6. Day 17's production task was reduced to a core bus question and answer, one follow-up about departure time, and optional ticket-purchase/role-switch extensions.

## What this audit does not establish

- It does not verify that every dialogue has been heard as actual audio.
- It does not certify pronunciation, listening comprehension, learner performance, or CEFR level.
- It does not establish a complete phrase-to-card mapping for the 1,000-card Anki deck.
- It is not a full line-by-line review of all lesson language, grammar, translations, answers, timing, rendering, or external resources.
- It does not mean all 90 lessons are approved or completed.
