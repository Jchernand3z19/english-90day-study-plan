# Voice pilot incident — 2026-10-29

## What happened

A short voice exercise was attempted on 2026-10-29. The tutor improvised a dialogue about “Alex” looking for a job in data analysis and then asked five comprehension/production questions. The learner asked whether answers should be in English or Spanish, received a model, and answered only the first three items before stopping because the activity felt difficult.

## Why this is not valid Day 1 evidence

The improvised job-search dialogue was not the assigned dialogue or activity in the repository's Day 1 lesson at that time. The task also moved into sentence production before the assigned language had been taught and modelled. The learner correctly pointed out that the actual lesson had not been followed.

Therefore this attempt:
- does **not** count as completion of Day 1;
- does **not** count as listening comprehension evidence for the Day 1 lesson;
- does **not** produce a valid score or diagnostic result;
- must not be interpreted as evidence that the learner failed the assigned Day 1 tasks.

The learner's stopping after three items is recorded as a process issue, not a learning score.

## Corrective action

Day 1 was rewritten as a short beginner lesson:
1. begin gently; one-word answers are acceptable;
2. teach and model a small set of personal sentence frames;
3. use the exact short listening text in lessons/day-001.md;
4. ask only the three comprehension questions in that file;
5. build the same three sentences aloud with support;
6. record which frames were independent, model-supported, or not yet attempted.

The listening text is: “Hello. My name is Alex. I'm from Canada. I live in Toronto.” The learner should hear it twice without seeing it first; the text may be revealed only afterward. If no audio is actually heard, listening must be marked **Not assessed**.

The study method and README were updated to require strict fidelity to the current lesson file, one question at a time, and teaching/modeling before unsupported production.
