# Phase and Final Assessments

Use these assessments after practising the corresponding phase. They measure practical performance for this study plan; they are not official CEFR examinations.

## General rules

- Attempt the speaking tasks aloud without reading a full script.
- For listening, have a tutor or text-to-speech tool read a supplied script without showing it first, or use a suitable audio clip and record its source. If listening cannot be assessed, mark it **Not assessed**; do not invent a score.
- The tutor may explain and correct in Spanish, but the learner should attempt answers in English first.
- Record the date, prompts, score, evidence, and corrections in a daily log.
- Suggested passing score: at least 80% overall, with all tasks attempted and the main message understandable.
- If not passed, mark **Review**, identify the weakest skills, and retry similar tasks after targeted practice. Do not invent a score or claim a task was completed when it was not.

## How to assign task points consistently

Use the maximum points printed beside each task; do not change weights after seeing a response.

- **20-point task:** score four dimensions from 0–5: completion of the stated requirements, accuracy/relevance of the message, understandable language, and independence/interaction.
- **25-point task:** score those four dimensions from 0–5, plus one task-specific dimension from 0–5 (for example, sequencing a story or asking relevant follow-up questions).
- **15-point task:** score three task-specific dimensions from 0–5, chosen from the requirements printed in that task. For a listening task, use main idea, detail accuracy, and completeness of answers; assess clarification/repair only when that task explicitly requires it.
- **Not attempted / not actually heard:** record **Not assessed**, not a performance score. If any required task is Not assessed, the checkpoint is **Incomplete** even if the remaining numeric subtotal is high.
- Minor grammar errors should reduce the score only when they affect the task requirement or meaning. Do not penalize a beginner for not sounding native; score understandable communication and the target skill.

Record one piece of evidence for each score. When evidence is too limited to justify a precise number, use a score range in the notes and keep the task **Not assessed** until a fair evaluation is possible.

## Phase 1 — Day 29: Foundations and daily life

### Task A — Personal introduction (20 points)
Speak for 45–60 seconds. Include your name, where you are from, where you live, work or daily activity, one interest, and why you are learning English.

### Task B — Everyday role-play (25 points)
Choose one: buy an item, order food, or ask for a service. Ask at least two questions and make one polite request.

### Task C — Directions and routine (20 points)
Ask for directions to a place and explain a simple route. Then describe three parts of a typical day.

### Task D — Listening (20 points)
Listen to the short beginner-level dialogue in Day 29, state the main idea, and answer its four detail questions. If using a tutor-read script, do not read it before answering. If you use a different audio clip, record its source and use three or four simple detail questions.

### Task E — Retrieval and clarification (15 points)
Produce five phrases from the phase without looking and use one clarification phrase appropriately.

**Total: 100 points.** Review focus: basic questions, present simple, polite requests, location words, and pronunciation of key phrases.

Day 30 is for corrections and reflection, not an automatic pass.

## Phase 2 — Day 59: Travel, socializing, and stories

### Task A — Travel scenario (20 points)
Ask about or confirm a fictional reservation, date, time, and one travel detail.

### Task B — Past experience (25 points)
Tell a 1–2 minute story. Include a beginning, at least three past actions, sequence words, and an ending. A clearly labelled fictional story is acceptable if needed.

### Task C — Unexpected problem (20 points)
Explain a travel or service problem, request help, and discuss one possible solution.

### Task D — Social conversation (20 points)
Maintain a guided conversation for 3–5 minutes. Answer questions and ask at least three relevant follow-up questions.

### Task E — Listening and repair (15 points)
Understand the main idea of a short dialogue and use a phrase to ask for repetition or clarification.

**Total: 100 points.** Review focus: past tense, sequence, follow-up questions, explaining problems, and polite requests.

Day 60 is for targeted corrections and a comparison with Phase 1 evidence.

## Phase 3 — Day 89: Fluency, discussion, and nuance

### Task A — Opinion with support (20 points)
Give an opinion on a familiar topic, explain two reasons, and provide an example.

### Task B — Conversation and response (20 points)
Respond to a different viewpoint respectfully and ask one follow-up question.

### Task C — Professional scenario (20 points)
Give a truthful professional introduction and answer two interview or networking questions using real experience.

### Task D — Listening and paraphrasing (20 points)
Listen to a short conversation that is only as challenging as your demonstrated level, with one or two new expressions at most. Summarise its main point and paraphrase one idea. A tutor-read script is acceptable if it is not shown before listening. Do not increase difficulty just because this is Phase 3.

### Task E — Conversation repair (20 points)
Respond to an unexpected question, ask for clarification if needed, and reformulate one answer more clearly.

**Total: 100 points.** Review focus: clarity, continuity, connectors, appropriate expressions, paraphrasing, and communication repair.

## Day 90 — Reflection and next plan

Answer honestly:

1. Which situations can I now handle more easily than on Day 1?
2. Which phrases can I retrieve and use without looking?
3. What still causes difficulty: listening speed, vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, or confidence?
4. What evidence supports my conclusion?
5. What three goals should I work on during the next 30 days?

Do not convert this internal score directly into a CEFR label. For an official level estimate, use an appropriate standardised assessment.
